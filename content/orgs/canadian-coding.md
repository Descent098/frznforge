Canadian Coding is the umbrella I publish under. It isn't a company and it isn't hiring —
it's a name to put on the things I keep maintaining after the itch that produced them has
been scratched, so that they read as a body of work rather than a scatter of one-off repos.

## What ties these together

- **Offline-first by default.** If a tool needs a server to be useful, that's a design
  decision I want to have made deliberately, not one I backed into.
- **Boring outputs.** Static files, SQLite, plain text. Formats that will still open in ten
  years without a vendor's permission.
- **Source-available.** Read it, clone it, fork it, take the good parts. Most of it exists
  because the alternative was heavier than the problem.

## Where the code lives

Everything here is mirrored from git repositories — this site is generated from them, not
the other way around. The canonical remotes live on GitHub; this forge is the read-only
window onto them, rebuilt from scratch on every deploy.
